The story of the hovercraft, which we named Cheburashka, began in September 2025. As part of the Introduction to Digital Manufacturing course, we were tasked with building a device with a non-standard design. That’s how I came up with the idea to build Cheburashka.

I didn’t want to copy anything from the internet, so I built it exactly as I had imagined it. By that point, I had already been working with drones and knew how to design models in Blender, so the development process got off to a quick start. Yes, Blender is the most user-friendly program for me, and it’s fairly easy to turn it into a decent CAD system using add-ons. But most importantly, Blender is very forgiving of mistakes, and that will save you more than once.

Components

All that I list below is everything that is in my Cheburashka, this is not a recommendation for purchase and you can find better modules. After the list, I'll tell you what can be improved by modules.

  • Cheburashka's Brains - ESP 32
  • ESC for rotary engine - ESC 50A BLHeli (x2)
  • ESC for main engine - ESC 80A BLHeli
  • Rotary engines - BAT-Surpass B2808 1500KV (x2)
  • Main engine - Motor 3115 900kv Spinner Production
  • GPS and Compas Module - Matek GPS & Compass M8Q-5883
  • Accelerometer and Gyro Module - MPU 6050 (for Arduino)
  • Radio transmitter (Cheburashka control) - FRSKY R9mini-OTA
  • Radio resiever (Cheburashka control) - R9M Module
  • Radio resiever (Cheburashka control) - R9M Module
  • Video transmitter and camera - Foxeer camera
  • Superimposing data on analog video - MINI OSD
  • Propellers - 3.5" (5 lumps, x2) & 5" (3 lumps)

So, now a few of my recommendations. Of course, the ESP32 must be equipped with a Wi-Fi module. I also advise you to purchase a pin expander for the board.

I recommend buying rotary motors with a characteristic of the order of 2000-3000 KV. It will be more comfortable for 3.5" diameter propellers. The main engine should be of the order of 1500-2000 KV.

There are much more interesting radio transmitters, but I still advise you to look at the information about the ExpressLRS firmware. This firmware is also suitable for FrSky R9 Mini OTA. Such a connection can operate at a distance of up to ~15 km, which ensures high-quality signal reception and safe operation.

The battery can be from 4S to 6S. When using the 4S, you will be able to install a larger battery. I also advise you to buy shrink wrap and nylon wires of different thicknesses.

M3 bolts are used. Buy nylon ones about 1.5 cm long — you can always cut them to the desired length — as well as metal ones about 1 cm long. Don't forget about nuts and 4-mm-high solder-in bushings

Assembly

Part 1: Assemble Model

So, to assemble the model, you’ll need 16 M3 nylon bolts, each 1.5 cm long. Why nylon? Because you can cut them to the desired length, which is very convenient. You’ll also need about 20 4-mm-tall solder-in bushings for M3 bolts.

First, you need to glue the bottom in place so that the body is airtight. For this, I used PU-40 polyurethane sealant.

Next, carefully solder the solder-in bushings in place using a soldering iron. I didn’t have a special tool for this, but you can use a long bolt, screw the solder-in bushing onto it, heat it with a soldering iron, and hold the bolt in place with pliers. I think it’s obvious where to solder them in, but I’ve marked the spots with red circles just in case.

Do the same carefully on top and in the braces.

Attach the lugs. I think it’s pretty clear how to position them to fit into the holes. Tighten the bolts onto the nuts. It’s quite possible that the M3 bolts won’t fit through the holes, and that’s perfectly normal—just drill them out if necessary.

Next, you need to screw on the motors. They usually come with bolts; I recommend screwing a nylon nut onto these bolts. During prolonged operation, they tend to heat up from the motor windings, and without you noticing, they can melt through the mounting point, so the nylon nut acts as a buffer.

Fit the main motor housing in place, aligning the holes so that the wires can be passed through them. Next, screw in the main motor, seal its housing, and seal the opening in the body where the wires pass through.

Next, attach the skeleton from below.

The two remaining parts are needed to clamp the pocket between them.

Success! You’ve assembled Cheburashka model.

Part 2: Assemble Electronics

So, you’ve assembled the Cheburashka model. Now let’s talk about the electronics.

I strongly recommend purchasing heat-shrink tubing in various diameters in advance. For the power wires, you’ll need XT60 connectors and wires with a fairly large gauge—I’m using wires with a diameter of about 3 mm. For connecting the modules, wires with a smaller gauge, about 1 mm, will suffice.

I soldered everything using a TS100 soldering iron. Also, buy solder flux and solder. For solder, I recommend MECHANIC TY-V866.

Below is a table and a wiring diagram. It’s hard to get confused here. Just look at the labels on the module and the ESP32. There’s no need to say anything more.

Firmware

Now let's talk about how to flash esp32. I recommend doing this on a Unix system.

  • Install the Arduino IDE.
  • Please read the article. You need to download the firmware_vars.json on ESP32.
  • Open the esp32_firmware.ino file in IDE.
  • Install the necessary libraries:
    • Adafruit_MPU6050
    • Adafruit_Sensor
    • DHT
    • QMC5883LCompass
    • TinyGPS++
    • ESP32Servo
    • Sbus
    • ArduinoJson
    • LittleFS
  • Compile it. If the compilation is successful, connect the esp32 and download the firmware on ESP32.
